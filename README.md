## What actually decides a good Los Angeles VPS

The route leaving the datacenter decides more than the "LA" label does. Physically, any Los Angeles server answers West Coast users fast: single-digit milliseconds for people inside the city, low tens across the region. Every credible LA provider has that part covered, and several of the providers competing for this keyword run well-known facilities — Coresite LA1 downtown, Digital Realty, QuadraNet on West 6th Street, and DataBank's One Wilshire, one of the most interconnected buildings in the country.

LA matters for a second reason. It's the landing point for most transpacific submarine cables, which makes it the natural departure city for traffic heading to Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia, Australia and mainland China. This is where providers separate. Ordinary international transit gets handed off to China Telecom's regular backbone, which congests badly during China's evening peak. CN2 GIA is China Telecom's premium commercial network, and traffic riding it keeps reserved capacity end to end.

That one difference explains the price spread in this search. Two LA offers with near-identical specs can cost $4 a month and $50 a year because one of them is built around the route. BandwagonHost's entire LA lineup is built around CN2 GIA, which is why its specs-per-dollar look ordinary next to monthly competitors and its behavior at 9 p.m. Beijing time doesn't.

## Order details people usually miss

Three things in BandwagonHost's fine print change how the plans behave day to day, and none of them appear in the spec tables.

**Traffic counts in both directions.** Inbound and outbound both deduct from the monthly quota, so a "2 TB" allowance behaves closer to 1 TB each way on a two-way-heavy workload. Most listings quote the headline number and stop there. Budget with the double-count in mind, especially if you push backups or sync jobs into the server.

**Hitting the cap suspends the network.** There's no soft throttle. When the quota is gone, network access stops until the next cycle resets it, or until you buy additional bandwidth from the KiwiVM panel. On a production box, set a traffic alert before you find this out at 2 a.m.

**The datacenter choice is reversible.** You pick the location at checkout, but you can migrate to any location your plan supports from the KiwiVM panel later at no extra cost. The trade is a new IP and some downtime while the disk copies, so if you're torn between DC6 and DC9, the decision isn't permanent.

No. These plans are price-locked — the rate at signup is the rate you renew at.
